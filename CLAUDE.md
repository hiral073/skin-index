# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## Project

Skin Index: Flask app for exploring skincare products (ingredients, price per ml, product comparison, shopper-engagement insights) with live-data pages (today's UV, humidity and air quality; ingredient science) and, later, ingredient tools. It informs and never recommends (see Claims and safety rules). Built step by step per the Roadmap below. Stack: Python, Flask, SQLite, pandas, plain HTML/CSS/JS, pytest.

## Commands (Windows PowerShell)

```
python -m venv venv
.\venv\Scripts\Activate.ps1
pip install -r requirements.txt
python -m database.load_data   # (re)load data/product_info.csv into skin_index.db; empties the 3 tables first
python app.py                  # dev server, http://localhost:5001 (debug=True)
pytest                         # all tests
pytest tests/test_routes.py::test_landing_page_loads   # single test
```

No linter or build step configured. Two virtualenvs exist in the tree (`venv/`, `.venv/`); README and `.gitignore` use `venv/`.

## Scope

MVP pages:
- Products, with search and category filter (the avoid-list exclude filter comes with Step 12c)
- Product detail
- Compare
- Insights

Live-data pages: Today (UV, humidity and US AQI as information, an attributed "Sun protection today" card and attributed weather-based skin-care tips) and Ingredient Science (PubMed; linked from the footer and the product pages, not the top navigation).

Later: an MCP server over the same data (Step 11), then Ingredient Intelligence (Step 12) and engineering polish (Step 13).

Still out of scope: login, an in-app AI assistant, Indian product data, product recommendations, and skin-type or skin-concern advice. INR is only a display conversion of the USD prices already in the dataset, not Indian product data.

## Architecture

- `app.py`: single Flask app, module-level `app`. `/` renders `landing.html`; `/products` is the real search page (`products.html`) and `/products/<int:id>` is the real detail page (`product_detail.html`). `/compare` is the real compare page (`compare.html`). `/insights` is the real insights page (`insights.html`). `/today` is the real Today page (`today.html`). No placeholder routes are left; later steps add routes, and templates/nav link to routes via `url_for('products')` etc., so keep the endpoint function names.
- `/insights?group=<name>`: computed from the database only (the one outside call is the cached rate service, for rupee values on product prices). It is about shopper engagement (likes, ratings, reviews from the retailer's site, not sales) and ingredients; there are no median or min/max price sections. Sections: overview tiles (products and brands; no comparison groups tile); most liked brands (top 10 by total `loves_count`, with product count and a review-weighted average rating; bars relative to the top brand; always overall); most liked products (top 10 by likes, overall or within the group: name linked to its detail page, brand, price in USD and `≈ ₹`, rating and reviews); group only: well-reviewed and lower price per ml (at least 50 reviews and a rating of at least 4.0, sorted by price per ml ascending, top 10, thresholds stated on the page) and price vs rating (products with a price per ml and at least 10 reviews split into three equal-size bands by price per ml, each with its review-weighted average rating and n, plus the note that this shows a pattern, not a cause; needs at least 9 such products, else "Not enough data"); common ingredients (top 10 by share of products, Water in a note); key takeaways; a "How to read this" note. The selector lists groups with at least 5 products (GET form, visible Show button, `data-autosubmit`); an unknown or too-small group shows the overall view with a small message. Products with no comparison group are excluded from group views. Bars are plain HTML/CSS with percentage widths computed in `app.py` (ratings on a 0-5 scale, ingredient shares on 0-100). Sections without data show "Not enough data".
- `/products/<int:id>`: name, brand, category, size, price in USD and `≈ ₹` (same rate service, rounding and note as the list), price per ml in USD and `≈ ₹` per ml, and the numbered ingredient list in label order (`position`); an ingredient with a stated strength shows "12.025% (stated by brand)" (the `percent` template filter), and a product with any shows the note "Only some brands state strengths. A missing percentage means not stated, not low." Anything missing shows "Not available". An unknown id gives a 404 page (`404.html`, extends `base.html`, link back to `/products`); the app-wide `@app.errorhandler(404)` renders it for every 404. The list links each name to the detail page and adds the current `q`, `category`, `sort` and `page` (defaults left out) to the link; the detail page rebuilds its "Back to results" link from only those four keys (`list_args()` in `app.py`), so unknown or invalid params are dropped.
- `/compare?group=<name>&ingredient=<key>&a=<id>&b=<id>`: ids must be plain positive integers, anything else is ignored. Two products are compared only if they have the same comparison group and share a main ingredient group (see Comparison rules). One GET form with four dropdowns in order: 1. Category (groups with at least two products), 2. Main ingredient (the groups present in the category, each with its product count; one with fewer than two products is disabled and labelled "too few to compare"), 3. Product A and 4. Product B, both listing only that category and main ingredient, each without the product chosen on the other side, labelled "Brand - Name (size, $price)". A later dropdown appears only once the ones before it are chosen; there is a visible "Show" button; `static/js/main.js` auto-submits dropdowns marked `data-autosubmit` and empties the fields below the one that changed (`data-resets`). With `?a=<id>` (the detail page link) the category comes from that product, and the ingredient dropdown lists only that product's main ingredient groups (one is chosen for you). A product left over from an earlier category or ingredient (`group`, `ingredient` in the URL not matching it) is treated as not chosen, so the page also works without JavaScript. Fewer than two products is said plainly: "There is only 1 product in X, so there is nothing to compare.", "Only 1 product in X is in the Y group, so there is nothing to compare.", "In X, no main ingredient group has 2 or more products, so there is nothing to compare.", "No product in X is in a main ingredient group, so there is nothing to compare." Both chosen and comparable: one table with Name, Brand, Category, Main ingredient, Size, `≈ Price (₹)`, `≈ Price per ml (₹)`, Likes, Rating, Reviews, then "Ingredients both have", "Ingredients only in A" and "Ingredients only in B" (names, with counts), the rate note, a line that likes, ratings and reviews come from one retailer's site as of March 2023, the note "Ingredient order is a rough guide. Amounts are not in our data.", the note "Ingredient names are compared ignoring capital letters, and Water, Aqua and Eau count as the same ingredient. No other names are matched or merged." and the sentence that shared ingredients do not mean the formulas behave the same. With no saved exchange rate the price rows are in USD with the note "Rupee prices are not available right now, so US dollar prices are shown." Stated strengths are not shown here (the note says amounts are not in our data; the detail page still shows them). There is no verdict, no "cheaper", no overlap percentage: no product is called better than another. Not comparable: a message ("Product A is in X and product B is in Y, so they can't be compared fairly."; "Product A is grouped under X and product B under Y, so they can't be compared here: they share no main ingredient group."; a product with no group or no main ingredient group gets its own message), both products' name, brand, category, size and price, and no per-ml, likes, ratings, reviews or ingredient rows. Unknown id and same id on both sides have their own message, missing values show "Not available". Never use the word "dupe". The detail page links to `/compare?a=<id>` and each `/products` row has a "Compare" link inside the product cell.
- `/products`: filters, sort and paging come from query args (`q`, `category`, `sort`, `page`; 24 per page). The database path is `app.config["DATABASE"]` (defaults to `database.db.DB_PATH`), so tests can point it at a temporary database. Columns: Product, Brand, Category, Size, Price (USD), and `≈ Price (₹)` (whole rupees, comma thousands separators) with a rate note under the table; with no rate, INR is not shown at all. Price per ml is not a column here, but it is still stored and still a sort option (`sort=price_per_ml`); `sort=price` sorts by the actual price.
- `templates/`: `base.html` (navbar, Google Fonts, `{% block title %}`, `{% block head %}`, content blocks) is extended by page templates.
- `database/db.py`: `get_db(db_path=None)` (Row factory, foreign keys on), `init_db()` (`CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS`), `seed_db()` (5 sample products, no-op if products exist). Tables: `products`, `ingredients`, `product_ingredients` (`position` = label order), `pubmed_searches` (saved Ingredient Science searches: cleaned search text, reviews-only flag, fetch time in UTC, parsed papers as JSON; see Ingredient Science rules), `pubmed_counts` (saved paper counts for the At a glance box: query text, count, fetch time in UTC), `exchange_rates` (`rate_date` = date the source publishes for, `fetched_on` = day we saved it; unique per base, quote, rate_date). `products.compare_group` is the comparison group (see Comparison rules); `products.loves_count`, `rating` and `reviews` (nullable) are the retailer's shopper engagement numbers. `init_db()` adds these columns, like `stated_percent`, to older databases (run the loader to fill them). Prices are stored in USD (`price`, `price_per_ml`). The loader empties only the three product tables, so saved rates survive a reload.
- `database/services.py`: service functions shared by pages and later the MCP server. `get_usd_inr_rate(db_path=None, today=None)` returns `RateInfo(rate, rate_date, stale)` or `None`: a rate fetched today is reused with no network call; otherwise it fetches and saves; if the fetch fails it returns the newest saved rate with `stale=True`. After a failed fetch it does not retry for 15 minutes, so an outage cannot slow every page load. It calls `init_db()` first so older databases get the `exchange_rates` table. `get_product_detail(product_id, db_path=None)` returns one product as a plain dict whose `ingredients` are `{"name", "stated_percent"}` dicts in label order (`stated_percent` is the brand's printed number or `None`, unformatted), or `None` for an unknown id; the MCP server should reuse it. `inr_price(price_usd, rate)` gives whole rupees (halves round up) and is what `/products` shows; `inr_per_ml(price_per_ml_usd, rate)` rounds to 2 decimals and is used by the detail and compare pages. Compare: `compare_products(a_id, b_id)` returns plain data only (both products with `loves_count`, `rating` and `reviews`, per-side state `not chosen`/`unknown`/`found`, a `status` of `waiting`/`same_product`/`no_group`/`different_groups`/`no_main_ingredient`/`different_ingredients`/`ok`, a `comparable` flag, both groups, each side's main ingredient groups `a_main`/`b_main`, `shared_main`, and, only when comparable, the shared / only-A / only-B ingredient names with counts; no verdict, no overlap, no stated strengths). `main_ingredients_of(product_name, ingredient_names)` puts a product in main ingredient groups from the rules in `content/main_ingredients.py`, computed at query time (no stored column, so no loader run is needed). `list_groups()`, `list_group_ingredients(group)` (the main ingredient groups present in a category with product counts) and `list_products_in_group(group, ingredient, exclude_id)` feed the dropdowns (the largest list is 80 products). `paper_label(types)` and `group_papers(papers)` give the Ingredient Science page's plain labels and groups. The MCP server should reuse all of these. `get_today_conditions(city=None, latitude=None, longitude=None, label=None)` returns the Today page's data as plain values, no HTML and no texts (status `ok`/`choose`/`no_match`/`invalid`/`unavailable`, place, choices, UV now and max with categories, humidity, temperature, AQI with category and PM2.5, `timeline` from `uv_timeline()`, `guidance` (`uv_card` of `full`/`low`/`very_low` from today's peak UV, `extra_steps` of `none`/`moderate_to_high`/`very_high`, `show_sunscreen_link`, `check_advisories`), `tips` (an empty group list, or `cool_dry` or `hot_humid`, and a headline), and a `missing` list). `TODAY_CONFIG` holds every threshold and category label: UV Low 0-2, Moderate 3-5, High 6-7, Very high 8-10, Extreme 11+; AQI Good 0-50, Moderate 51-100, Unhealthy for sensitive groups 101-150, Unhealthy 151-200, Very unhealthy 201-300, Hazardous 301+; sun-protection start UV 3, stronger steps UV 8, advisory above AQI 100; cool at or below 15 C, dry at or below 35%, hot at or above 30 C, humid at or above 60%, very-low UV below 1. UV and AQI are rounded half up to whole numbers before they get a category or are compared. `clean_place_text` accepts only letters, digits, spaces, hyphens, apostrophes, commas and full stops (2 to 60 characters; links, markup and domain-like text are refused) and `parse_coordinates` accepts only finite in-range numbers. The MCP weather tool should reuse it. `search_research(term, reviews_only=False, db_path=None, now=None)` returns the Ingredient Science page's data as plain values (status `ok`/`empty`/`invalid`/`unavailable`, the cleaned term and query, up to 10 papers `{pmid, title, journal, year, types, url}`, `source` `live`/`cache`/`stale_cache`, `fetched_at`); `clean_search_text` applies the city-text rules and lower-cases (so a typed AND or OR is not an operator). `get_ingredient_card(term)` returns an ingredient's card as plain data (`key`, `what`, `discussed` with their texts, source names and links, `checked`) or `None`; it makes no call; the MCP server should reuse it. `get_ingredient_glance(term, db_path=None, now=None)` returns the At a glance box's topic counts as plain data (status `ok`/`none`/`invalid`/`unavailable`, `topics` as `{topic, count}` sorted by count, `incomplete`, `fetched_at`); `ok` only when at least one topic has papers, and no topic is searched when there are no papers; the MCP server should reuse it. `RESEARCH_CONFIG` holds the 24 hour reuse window, 30 day pruning, the 1,000 row cap of saved searches, the 5,000 row cap of saved counts and the limit of 2 failed counts in a row. The MCP research tool should reuse it. `get_insights(group=None, db_path=None)` returns the numbers for the Insights page as plain data (no formatting, HTML or currency): `config` (`INSIGHTS_CONFIG`, the one block of thresholds: 5 products per group, top 10, 50 reviews and rating 4.0 for the well-reviewed list, 10 reviews and 9 products for the price bands), overview, selector groups, brands (overall), liked products (overall or in the group), and for a valid group `well_reviewed` and `price_bands`, plus ingredient shares with Water apart; `group_status` is `none`, `ok` or `unavailable`. `weighted_rating(pairs)` is sum(rating × reviews) ÷ sum(reviews), skipping products missing either value. A product missing a value a section needs is skipped by that section, never counted as zero. Ties are broken by name; bands are equal in size (the extra product goes to the lower bands first, equal prices ordered by id). The takeaway sentences are built in `app.py` from that data.
- `live/currency.py`: `fetch_usd_inr()` is the only code that calls the network for rates (Frankfurter `https://api.frankfurter.dev/v1/latest`, no key, `requests`, 5 second timeout). It raises `CurrencyError` with a plain message on any failure. Frankfurter lists v1 as deprecated but available indefinitely (v2 is the documented current API); only this function needs to change if v1 goes away.
- `/today?city=<text>` or `?lat=&lon=&label=`: live conditions for a city (see Today rules). With no arguments it shows the default place, Pune, Maharashtra, India, from fixed coordinates in `TODAY_CONFIG` (no lookup, so no choice list on first load). A city name is looked up with the Open-Meteo geocoding API: one match shows its conditions, several (up to 5) show name, region and country as links (`/today?lat=..&lon=..&label=..`), none shows a friendly message. Tiles (information only, no advice): UV now and today's max with their category, humidity, temperature, US AQI with its category and PM2.5; AQI above Moderate adds only "Check your local health advisories". Once a place is resolved (even if the live services are down) the page shows, in this order: (1) "Skin-care tips for today's weather", only when the weather fits a group, a cool-or-dry group (temperature at or below 15 C, or humidity at or below 35%; link "Browse moisturizers with 'cream' in the name" to `/products?category=Moisturizers&q=cream`) or a hot-and-humid group (30 C or above and humidity 60% or above; links to the Cleansers and Moisturizers categories), each tip with its source label; there are no every-day tips, and a missing temperature or humidity never triggers a group; (2) "Sun protection today", driven by today's peak UV as a whole number: 3 or higher shows the UV timeline (the hours with UV 3 or higher as ranges, labelled by the hour each hourly value covers because Open-Meteo hourly values average the hour that ENDS at their time) and the peak hour, the American Academy of Dermatology sunscreen sentence once, the WHO extra steps ("seek shade, cover up, hat, sunscreen" at 3 to 7; "avoid being outside at midday where possible..." at 8 and above), the four "Also worth remembering" reminders and "Browse sunscreens" (`/products?category=Sunscreen`) with a note to check SPF and broad-spectrum; 1 to 2 shows only the one line "UV is low right now. Dermatologists still recommend daily sunscreen on exposed skin when you're outdoors." with its source (no timeline, peak, reminders, WHO steps or link); below 1 shows only "UV is very low today."; no UV data shows only "The UV index isn't available right now."; (3) one small note, "General information from the sources named. Not medical advice." The sunscreen sentence appears at most once on the page and no line says sun protection is unnecessary. If the services are down or slow the page says so and shows no numbers; if only some values are missing, the rest are shown. The page also carries "Live conditions, general information only. Not medical or dermatological advice." and the attributions ("Weather data by Open-Meteo.com", CAMS for air quality, GeoNames for places).
- `/science?q=<text>&reviews=1`: the Ingredient Science page (called Research until it was renamed): lists published papers about a skincare ingredient from PubMed (see Ingredient Science rules). It is not in the top navigation: it is linked from the footer under a small "Learn more" heading and from the product detail page (one "Studies on <ingredient>" link per main ingredient group the product is in, to `/science?q=<search term>`; none for a product in no group). `/research` is a 301 redirect to `/science` that keeps `q` and `reviews` and drops other parameters. A search box, an "Overview papers and combined analyses only" checkbox (`reviews=1`) and quick links for niacinamide, retinol, vitamin C, hyaluronic acid, salicylic acid and ceramides. Every search is "<ingredient> AND skin" and the page shows that query. Results (the 10 most relevant) are small cards grouped under plain labels, in this order, empty groups left out: "Start here: overview papers" (Review), "Studies combined into one analysis" (Systematic Review or Meta-Analysis), "Trials with people" (Randomized Controlled Trial or Clinical Trial) and "Other research papers" (anything else). A paper takes the first label in the content file's list that one of its PubMed types fits, so a Review that is also a Systematic Review is a combined analysis; inside a group PubMed's relevance order is kept. A card shows the title (cut at a word boundary to about 120 characters with an ellipsis, the full title in `title=` for hover), journal, year and a "Read on PubMed" link; no technical publication types, authors or PubMed codes are shown. Titles are quoted verbatim and escaped. The note sits above the results, word for word: "Listing of published papers. A study existing does not mean a product works. Source: PubMed (US National Library of Medicine)." followed by one line explaining the labels (they name the kind of paper, taken from PubMed's own record, and are not our summary of what a paper found). Both show only when papers are listed. Above the groups sits an "At a glance" box, in one of two forms. An ingredient with a card in `content/ingredient_cards.py` (niacinamide, retinol, retinal, vitamin C, hyaluronic acid, salicylic acid, ceramides; found by name or alias such as nicotinamide, ascorbic acid, retinaldehyde, hyaluronan, ceramide) shows the card: two short lines, "What it is" (a neutral fact) and "Often discussed in relation to" (1 to 3 topics taken only from the named source), each with its own named source and link (`Source: <name>`, `rel="noopener"`), and under them, word for word: "General information from the sources named. Not medical advice." The text is our own short wording; a line without a reliable source is left out of the card. A carded ingredient asks PubMed for no counts (2 PubMed calls for the papers only). An ingredient without a card shows the topic-count box when the counts are available, and no box otherwise: the heading "Often mentioned together with" and, for each topic in `GLANCE_TOPICS` (acne, hyperpigmentation, skin aging, skin barrier, irritation, rosacea, eczema), one count for `<ingredient> AND "<topic>"`; only topics with at least one paper are listed, most papers first (ties in the order of the content file); there are no totals, no overview count and no year range. Under that list, word for word: "These numbers show how often topics appear in published papers. They do not show that an ingredient works or is suitable for anyone. Source: PubMed (US National Library of Medicine)." The counts do not depend on the "Overview papers and combined analyses only" checkbox. With no topic count to show (no papers, no topic has a paper, every count failed, or only an old saved copy of the papers is shown) the counts box, note included, is left out. A topic count that fails is left out and the rest are shown; after 2 failed counts in a row the rest are not tried. Counts use the 24 hour cache and the PubMed rate limit; a cold first view of an ingredient without a card makes 9 PubMed calls (2 for the papers, 7 for the topics) and can take several seconds. Neither box shows when there are no papers. No papers shows "No papers found for ..."; PubMed down with nothing saved shows "Research search is unavailable right now."; PubMed down with an older saved copy shows it with "PubMed could not be reached, so these are saved results from <date>." The NCBI Disclaimer and Copyright link is always on the page. Counts appear only in the At a glance box (see Ingredient Science rules).
- `live/pubmed.py`: the only code that calls PubMed's E-utilities (`esearch` for IDs, `esummary` for details; `requests`, `params=`, 5 second timeout, `PubmedError` with a plain message). No API key; it sends `tool=skin-index`, and `email` only when the `NCBI_EMAIL` environment variable is set (read at search time, never written in the code or the repo; unset, blank or not an address means no email parameter). One process-wide limiter keeps at least 0.4 s between any two requests (NCBI allows 3 per second); a 429 is retried once after its `Retry-After` (capped at 2 s); after a failure PubMed is left alone for 10 s. "Reviews only" adds `(review[pt] OR systematic review[pt])` and keeps only papers whose types include Review or Systematic Review (PubMed's filter lets other types through), so it asks for 20 to list 10. Types are shown informative-first; "Journal Article" only when it is all there is; "Research Support" types are dropped. Only simple formatting tags (`<i>`, `<sub>` ...) are removed from titles. `count_pubmed(query)` makes one esearch call with `retmax=0` and returns the number of papers; it shares the limiter, timeout and 429 handling but not the 10 s failure memory (so one failed topic does not block the others). `topic_query` builds the count query for a topic. `reset_state()` and the module clock/sleep exist for tests.
- `content/science.py`: the fixed texts of the Ingredient Science page (the note, the label line, the plain labels and their order, the At a glance texts and the fixed topic list, messages, quick-link ingredients). Never use safe, best for, treats, cures, proven or effective here. `content/ingredient_cards.py`: the At a glance cards (two lines per ingredient, each with a named source, a link and a `checked` date) and the card note; never use safe, best for, treats, cures, proven, effective, helps or "is for", and never name a skin type. `content/main_ingredients.py`: the eleven main ingredient groups of the Compare page (niacinamide, ascorbic acid, retinol, retinal, hyaluronic acid, salicylic acid, glycolic acid, lactic acid, azelaic acid, ceramide and peptide; each matches one exact whole word or phrase, ignoring capital letters, and no related form or derivative is grouped with it), each with a key, a label, the name and ingredient patterns that put a product in it, and the search term of its "Studies on ..." link; `TOP_N = 10`.
- `content/today_tips.py`: the one file holding every fixed guidance and tip text on the Today page with its source (American Academy of Dermatology, World Health Organization, Johns Hopkins Medicine), the UV low and very-low lines, the headlines, the sources note and the browse links' labels and filters. Texts are quoted from their sources; do not add or reword one without the content owner agreeing, and never add a tip without a source. The every-day tips and the "see a board-certified dermatologist" line were removed on purpose and must not come back. `tests/test_today.py` pins every text and source as a literal, so an edit here fails a test.
- `live/weather.py`: the only code that calls Open-Meteo for conditions (`search_places`, `fetch_forecast`, `fetch_air_quality`; `requests` with `params=` and a 5 second timeout; `WeatherError` with a plain message). UV is requested as a current value (`uv_index`), the hourly `uv_index` (each value is the average of the hour ENDING at its time) and the daily `uv_index_max`, in one request with `timezone=auto`. Values outside plausible ranges become missing, never repaired. In-memory cache: 15 minutes per place or grid point (coordinates rounded to 2 decimals), a failure remembered for 10 seconds (so reloads in an outage are not hammered, yet a slow lookup can be retried quickly), at most 200 entries; `clear_cache()` and the module clock `_clock` exist for tests. The geocoding service returns no `results` key at all when nothing matches.
- `database/load_data.py`: pandas loader for `data/product_info.csv` (Skincare rows only; sizes converted to `size_ml`; ingredient lists split into the two ingredient tables; drop reasons printed). Cleaning helpers (`parse_size_ml`, `split_ingredients`, `parse_strength`, `parse_ingredients`, `clean_products`) are unit-tested without the CSV. It also reads `tertiary_category` to set each product's `compare_group` (see Comparison rules) and prints a group report (fallbacks, products with no group, groups with one product, renamed groups), and reads `loves_count`, `rating` and `reviews` (blank stays NULL) with a report of how many are filled and their ranges.
- Stated strengths (`product_ingredients.stated_percent`, nullable, must be above 0 and below 100; `init_db()` adds the column to older databases): for Skincare products outside the secondary categories "Value & Gift Sets" and "Mini Size" only, `parse_strength` splits a percentage the brand printed off an ingredient piece in exactly three formats: trailing (`Salicylic Acid 2.0%`, `... 15 %`), parenthesised (`Zinc Oxide (12.025%)`, `Ceramide Np(0.2%)`) and leading (`3% Procapil`). The cleaned name then goes through the Water alias map, so `Zinc Oxide 20%` and `Zinc Oxide` are one ingredient. Ignored and left exactly as written: values of 100 or more or 0 or below, and colon-style compounds (`Name : 0.0408%`). Anything else containing a `%` is ambiguous and also left unchanged: a word after the %, glued text (`6.90%Water`), several strengths in one piece, a range (`13.20 - 14.00 %`), a limit (`less than 1%`), a name that is part of a sentence or heading. When an ingredient name repeats, the first position is kept and takes the strength if it has none. The loader prints a report (counts, 30 varied examples, every ambiguous case, merged duplicates). Gift-set and mini-size products keep their raw names, so a `%` can still sit inside an ingredient name there.
- `data/`: `data/*.csv` and `data/*.parquet` are gitignored, as is `skin_index.db`. The CSV must be present locally to run the loader.
- `tests/conftest.py`: inserts project root into `sys.path`, imports `app` from `app.py`, and defines the `app` fixture (sets `TESTING=True` and gives each test its own empty temporary database via `app.config["DATABASE"]`). An autouse fixture replaces `live.currency.requests.get` with a call that fails like an outage, so no test reaches the network and by default no rate exists; the `rate_api` fixture patches it with a controllable fake; the `pubmed_api` fixture answers like PubMed's esearch and esummary (records, one-shot 429s, errors) and records every call, and the autouse `pubmed_clock` replaces the PubMed module's clock and sleep so no test really waits; the `weather_api` fixture answers like the three Open-Meteo services (geocoding, forecast, air quality; anything else stays offline) and records every call, and `weather_clock` moves the weather cache's clock. The weather cache is cleared before every test. `pytest-flask` supplies the `client` fixture from it. `test_routes.py` asserts every page (`/`, `/products`, `/compare`, `/insights`, `/today`, `/science`) loads on an empty database; add new pages to that list.

## Architecture rules

- All external HTTP calls live in a `live/` package and use `requests`. Every call has a timeout, a cache, and a clear error message the page can show.
- Routes and MCP tools never call external APIs directly. They call service functions.
- Tests never use the real network. Fake or stub the `live/` functions.
- SQL is parameterized only. Never build SQL text from request or tool input.
- Sort columns come from a whitelist of known keys; an unknown key falls back to the default.

## Data rules

- Data note: the product data is a March 2023 Sephora US snapshot. Prices, likes, ratings and reviews are as of then, not live.
- Prices are stored in USD.
- INR is derived at display time from the cached daily exchange rate. It is labeled approximate and shows the rate date.
- If today's rate cannot be fetched, use the last saved rate with its date. If no rate was ever saved, hide INR.
- Ingredient names are never merged: no normalizer, alias table or fuzzy matching (Step 12a was dropped). The only name matching, in the loader and so on the Compare lists, is trimming, ignoring capital letters, and treating Water, Aqua and Eau (as the only words in a name) as the one ingredient "Water". Every other spelling, derivative or related form is its own ingredient. The compare page says so in a note.
- Ingredient strengths are shown only when the brand printed them on the label: stored as `stated_percent`, shown as "12.025% (stated by brand)". Never infer, estimate or compute a concentration (not from list position, not from other products), and never treat a missing percentage as low or zero. Ambiguous text is left unchanged and reported, not guessed.

## Comparison rules

- A product's comparison group is the most specific category level the data has: the third level (`tertiary_category`, for example "Face Serums"), else the second level (`secondary_category`, for example "Lip Balms & Treatments"). Two products are comparable only if they have the same group.
- Mini Size and Value & Gift Sets products have no group: they mix every kind of product, so they are never compared (the page says they can't be compared fairly). No Value & Gift Sets product is in the database today, because the size rules dropped them all.
- Group cleanup: after the groups are built, `content/compare_groups.py` merges or hides some (`GROUP_MERGES`, `HIDDEN_GROUPS`), applied by the loader (`apply_group_cleanup` in `database/load_data.py`) so a rebuilt database gives the same result. Face Sunscreen and Body Sunscreen become the subcategories "Sunscreen › Face" and "Sunscreen › Body" (the one no-third-level Sunscreen product has no subcategory); For Face and For Body become "Self Tanners › Face" and "Self Tanners › Body"; Cleansers (the no-third-level fallback) is "Face Wash & Cleansers"; Eye Masks is "Eye Creams & Treatments"; Night Creams stays its own group. A subcategory is a group of its own for Compare and Insights (a face and a body sunscreen are not compared together); `group_sections()` in `app.py` and `templates/_group_options.html` render a parent with subcategories as one `<optgroup>` in the Products, Compare and Insights dropdowns, and the Products one adds "All <parent>" (a parent value also matches its subcategories). The two Anti-Aging groups, Holistic Wellness and Teeth Whitening get no group. Makeup Removers and BB & CC Creams stay separate on purpose. The `category` column is never changed and no product is deleted; before and after counts are in `docs/data-audit.md`. Add a group to the map there, not in code. The `/products` "Category" filter and column use the cleaned group (query arg still `category`; a product with no group shows "—" and is in no filter option, only in the unfiltered list); the original `category` is shown only on the product detail page. The Today browse links use group names ("Browse cleansers" goes to "Face Wash & Cleansers").
- A third-level name that appears under more than one second-level category is made unique by putting the second level in front, for example "High Tech Tools › Anti-Aging" and "Shop by Concern › Anti-Aging" (today the only such name). Names are checked across the whole Skincare CSV, so a group's name does not change when some of its products are dropped.
- A fallback product whose second-level name equals a third-level name joins that group (the one Moisturizers product with no third level is in "Moisturizers"). Groups with fewer than two products are not offered in the picker.
- Two products are comparable only if they have the same group and share a main ingredient group (`content/main_ingredients.py`: niacinamide, ascorbic acid, retinol, retinal, hyaluronic acid, salicylic acid, glycolic acid, lactic acid, azelaic acid, ceramide and peptide; each is one exact whole word or phrase matched ignoring capital letters, so retinol and retinal are two groups, vitamin C is only "ascorbic acid" (not ascorbyl or ascorbate names), "hyaluronic acid" does not include sodium hyaluronate, and "Tripeptide-1" is not "peptide", while "Ceramide NP" is in "ceramide" because it contains the whole word). A product is in a main ingredient group if the ingredient is in its name or among its first 10 listed ingredients; it can be in several. This is a rough guide to what a product lists near the top of its label, not a claim about what it does. Today 556 of 1,748 products are in a (group, main ingredient) combination with at least two products, so about 68% of products cannot be compared (590 are in a main ingredient group at all) and cannot be compared. A combination with fewer than two products is not offered, and the page says so.
- No product is ever called better, cheaper or the winner: the page shows each product's numbers side by side, with no verdict and no overlap percentage. Ingredient order is a rough guide and amounts are not in the data, so stated strengths are not shown on the Compare page. Shared ingredients are not a claim that two formulas behave the same.

## Insights rules

- Popularity means shopper engagement from one retailer's site: likes, reviews and ratings. There is no sales data. Say "most liked" or "most reviewed"; never "best-selling", "most sold", "bestseller" or anything about sales. Tests check the takeaways against a list of such words.
- Everything on the page is computed from the data and worded neutrally: numbers, never claims about effectiveness or quality. The price-vs-rating bands show a pattern, not a cause.
- Weighted average rating = sum(rating × reviews) ÷ sum(reviews). Ratings with few reviews are ignored where it matters: the well-reviewed list needs at least 50 reviews and a rating of at least 4.0, the price bands at least 10 reviews. Old products have had more time to collect likes and reviews, and the page says so.
- No median or min/max price sections on this page. Water is shown in a note, not in the ingredient ranking; its share is "at least" because names like "Purified Water" are separate ingredients.

## Claims and safety rules

- The app informs and never recommends. No product is called better, best, right or suitable for anyone.
- No skin-type, skin-concern or "good for" claims anywhere (pages, takeaways, MCP tools, docs).
- Never say "safe", "treats" or "cures". Wording is "contains ...".
- The user's avoid list lives only in the browser (the URL or local storage). It is never saved in the database and never logged.
- "Not flagged" must never be shown as "safe": an ingredient list with no match only means nothing on the list was found.
- Every page that shows ingredient information or the avoid list carries a patch-test / see-a-dermatologist note.
- Regulatory restrictions (for example EU CosIng) are shown only as a pointer to CosIng ("has an EU restriction, see CosIng"), never interpreted.
- One narrow exception, by decision of the project owner, for the Ingredient Science page's "At a glance" box. (1) Cards (`content/ingredient_cards.py`): for seven ingredients, a "What it is" line and an "Often discussed in relation to" line (1 to 3 topics, which may name skin conditions such as acne or eczema), each taken only from its named source (MedlinePlus, ChEBI, NLM MeSH, American Academy of Dermatology, Cleveland Clinic), shown with the source's name and link and the line "General information from the sources named. Not medical advice." Our wording only names what a source discusses: never "is for", "treats", "cures", "helps", "best for", "safe", "proven" or "effective", no skin types, no advice, and a line with no reliable source is left out. (2) Counts, for ingredients without a card: counts of PubMed papers, including how many papers mention the ingredient together with a topic. These are counts, not claims, always with the line "These numbers show how often topics appear in published papers. They do not show that an ingredient works or is suitable for anyone. Source: PubMed (US National Library of Medicine)." The heading is "Often mentioned together with", never "helps with" or "used for". The exception covers only the texts in `content/ingredient_cards.py` and the topic list in `content/science.py`; those words must not appear anywhere else, and the Step 13 claims scan must allow them only there. Every card entry must keep at least one source with a link; `tests/test_ingredient_cards.py` pins every card text and checks it for banned wording.
- One narrow exception, by decision of the project owner: the Today page's tips (Step 9). They are quoted word for word from named public sources, each shown with its source label and the line "General information from the sources named. Not medical advice.", so they are not the app's own claims. They include wording such as "tend to suit dry, sensitive skin" and "more effective ... for dry skin" because the sources say so. The exception covers only the texts in `content/today_tips.py`; everywhere else, and for any new text, the rules above apply in full, and the Step 13 claims scan must allow only that file's texts.

## Today rules

- Information first: live UV, humidity, temperature and US AQI. The tiles carry no advice; AQI above Moderate adds only "check local health advisories".
- All guidance and tips are quoted from named public sources (American Academy of Dermatology, World Health Organization, Johns Hopkins Medicine), shown with their source labels, and live only in `content/today_tips.py`. The weather-based tips apply by config thresholds (cool or dry: at or below 15 C or at or below 35% humidity; hot and humid: at or above 30 C and 60% humidity); with a missing reading no group applies, and with no group there is no tips card. The sunscreen advice appears at most once on the page (the full sentence at UV 3 or higher, the one low line at 1 to 2, none below 1). No line may say sun protection is unnecessary or "not needed" at low UV. Never use "safe", "best for", "treats" or "cures". Tests check the whole page and the content file for banned wording.
- Links go only to category lists on the Products page (sunscreens; moisturizers with "cream" in the name; cleansers; moisturizers). They are links, not recommendations; no product is named or ranked.
- Never invent a value: a missing or implausible value is shown as not available, and a service that is down or slow gives a friendly message.
- Open-Meteo's free tier is for non-commercial use only, with limits of 10,000 calls a day, 5,000 an hour and 600 a minute; the cache keeps this app far below them. A site with ads or subscriptions would need their paid plan. The data is CC-BY 4.0, so keep the attributions on the page.

## Ingredient Science rules

- The page lists published papers only: title, journal, year, a plain label for the kind of paper ("Start here: overview papers", "Studies combined into one analysis", "Trials with people" or "Other research papers") and a link to PubMed. It never says an ingredient works, is safe or is good for anything, writes no summary of a paper and shows no abstracts, authors or technical codes. Counts appear only in the At a glance box.
- One note above the results, word for word: "Listing of published papers. A study existing does not mean a product works. Source: PubMed (US National Library of Medicine)." Then one line saying the groups name the kind of paper and are not our summary. The At a glance box carries its own line (see Claims and safety rules). No other health claims.
- Banned wording on this page (our own text, in `content/science.py`, the template and the tests): safe, best for, treats, cures, proven, effective. Paper titles are PubMed's text, shown verbatim, and may contain such words; the banned-word tests skip the result list.
- Stay under 3 requests per second to PubMed (0.4 s minimum gap), keep the 5 second timeout, and keep NCBI's Disclaimer and Copyright link on the page. Never write a contact email in the code or the repo: it comes from the `NCBI_EMAIL` environment variable, and with none set no email is sent.
- Saved searches (`pubmed_searches`) hold only the cleaned search text, the reviews-only flag, the fetch time and the parsed papers: no user identity and no raw PubMed data. A copy under 24 hours old is reused with no PubMed call; an older copy is shown only if PubMed cannot be reached, with its date. Rows older than 30 days are deleted and at most 1,000 are kept. Saved counts (`pubmed_counts`) hold only the query text, the count and the time, are reused for 24 hours (a failed count is never saved), are pruned at 30 days and capped at 5,000 rows. The product loader never touches these tables.

## MCP rules

- MCP tools call the same service functions as the pages.
- Tools: search, compare, insights, weather and the avoid-list check. There is no skin-type tool.
- The avoid-list check takes the list as an argument for that one call and does not store it (see Claims and safety rules).
